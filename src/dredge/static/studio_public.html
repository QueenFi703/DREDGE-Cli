Public architecture

From source record to reviewed casework

  1. Casework evidence

    Original documents are preserved. Local OCR drafts require staff verification. Source-linked timeline entries retain dates, observed amounts and page references.

  2. Clarification and client voice

    Record discrepancies as neutral questions and consider client explanations alongside the evidence. These records do not calculate official balances or authorize enforcement.

  3. Independent packet review

    Packets retain immutable snapshots of evidence, explanations and drafts. A different authorized staff member verifies the current snapshot. Draft release and qualified legal review remain separate controls.

  4. Identity and roles

    An enabled identity provider establishes identity. Server-assigned viewer, operator, reviewer, and admin roles control access.

  5. Run proposal

    An operator frames the question and attaches source records. A separate reviewer approves or rejects the immutable proposal.

  6. Local DAG execution

    Approved requests execute the standard or iOS pipeline. Each actual node transition records its dependencies, timing, status, and execution mode.

  7. Trace and evidence

    The graph is built from stored node events. Source records link to their original URLs and disclose that they were supplied by a user and have not been independently verified.

  8. Audit and reporting

    SQLite retains runs and hash-linked review events. Reliability uses completed and failed local runs; unmetered cost and tokens remain unavailable.

MACSS integration is a proposed next phase requiring agency authorization and interface specifications. No official records or payments are changed by this pilot. OpenAI inference and public web research are separate, gated capabilities.
